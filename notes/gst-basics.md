# GST Basics (my notes)

## 1. Who bears GST
The final consumer bears GST. The supplier collects it and pays it to the government.

## 2. HSN and SAC codes
- HSN is a classification code for goods. SAC is the same for services.
- They are not the GST registration number. That is called the GSTIN.
- The GST rate depends on the code, so many rate questions are really classification questions.

## 3. Exempt vs nil-rated vs zero-rated
- Nil-rated: the rate in the rate schedule is 0%, so no GST is charged.
- Exempt: the item would normally be taxable, but a notification exempts it. The government can withdraw the exemption later.
- Both are exempt supplies, so a business making them generally cannot claim input tax credit on related purchases.
- Zero-rated (for example exports): no GST is charged, but input tax credit is still allowed.

## 4. Input tax credit (ITC)
ITC is the GST a business already paid on its purchases. It subtracts this from the GST it collects on its sales and pays only the difference to the government.

Example:
- Buys raw material for Rs 1,000 + 18% GST = Rs 180 (this is the ITC)
- Sells the product for Rs 1,500 + 18% GST = Rs 270 collected
- Pays the government 270 - 180 = Rs 90

This is why GST is a tax on value added at each stage, and the final consumer ends up bearing it.
